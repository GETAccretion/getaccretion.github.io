Install iPhone apps without a computer

The short version: a paid signing certificate lets your iPhone sign and install apps by itself for about a year. No computer, no weekly refresh.

What sideloading is

Sideloading means installing an iPhone app that isn't from the App Store. iOS only runs apps that are signed, so every sideloaded app has to be signed with a certificate before it will open. Normally that signing happens on a computer, which is why most guides tell you to plug your phone into a PC or Mac. You can skip the computer, but you need the right kind of certificate.

Your options

A paid signing certificate

You buy a certificate from a seller. It comes as a .p12 file, a .mobileprovision file and a password. You add it to a signer app on your iPhone, and the phone signs and installs apps itself.

  • Computer needed: no. You can do it all from the phone.
  • Lasts: about a year.
  • Cost: paid, set by the seller.
  • Risk: Apple can revoke a certificate. Good sellers replace it.

Free AltStore or SideStore

These use your own Apple ID instead of a bought certificate.

  • Computer needed: yes, once, for the first setup.
  • Lasts: 7 days. Apps must be refreshed every week.
  • Cost: free.
  • Limit: a free Apple ID allows only a few sideloaded apps at once.

If you only want to try one app, the free route is fine. If you want to install many apps, without a computer and without refreshing every week, a paid certificate is the simpler path.

If Apple revokes your certificate

Apple sometimes revokes certificates. When that happens, apps signed with it stop opening. Sellers like Signulous replace a revoked certificate. Install your apps again through the seller, or, if you sign in Accretion, add the new files there and use Re-sign All from the ✨ menu in Library.

Questions

Can I sideload iOS apps without a computer?

Yes. With a paid certificate, you install a signer app once and then sign and install other apps from the iPhone. The free AltStore and SideStore route needs a computer for the first setup.

What is an iOS signing certificate?

It is a set of files (a .p12 certificate, a .mobileprovision profile and a password) that lets an app signer sign an IPA so iOS accepts it. Paid ones last about a year.

Is it safe?

Accretion doesn't review third-party apps, so install only what you trust. The certificate only signs apps; keep its files private.

What happens if Apple revokes my certificate?

Apps stop opening until you add a replacement. See the section above.

Do I need to jailbreak?

No. Sideloading uses Apple's normal signing system and works on a stock iPhone.
